01 / Perspective

An interface is an
information choice.

Language models increasingly supply the predictions on which other systems act. The important question is what a report means—and whether it supports the decision that follows.

We connect classification, reward and quality scoring, numerical prediction, and probabilistic forecasting through three questions.

  1. 01

    What is the target?

    A ranking score, an outcome probability, and a teacher’s quantile make different claims.

  2. 02

    What survives the transformation?

    Preserving order, enforcing a constraint, and improving task performance are different achievements.

  3. 03

    Where is the computation?

    A compact report may require repeated generation—or reuse work done earlier.

02 / The taxonomy

Different reports.
Different commitments.

Follow what reaches the consumer, then how it is produced. These roles can overlap: one system may expose quantiles, a median, and a thresholded decision.

DWhich option?

Decisions

A selected class, candidate, route, or action under a specified rule.

Representative routes
  • Candidate-token readouts
  • Joint classifiers & routers

Select a model before an answer is generated.

SHow does it rate?

Scores

Values used to compare, rank, or threshold candidates against a criterion.

Representative routes
  • Likelihood-based scoring
  • Ratings & reward readouts

Rank candidate responses by reward.

VWhat value?

Numerical estimates

A point or numerical property defined by a target, units, and loss.

Representative routes
  • Scalar or iterative heads
  • Sample-based statistics

Predict a median price for absolute error.

PWhat uncertainty?

Distributions & summaries

A predictive law, samples, or specified uncertainty summaries for a named target.

Representative routes
  • Numerical-bin distributions
  • Quantile heads & probes

Retain price quantiles for a tail-sensitive decision.

C

Structured composition

A cross-cutting boundary: combine local reports into spans, fields, or constrained assignments.

Across the four roles

Finite quantiles do not specify a full density. A teacher’s generation process and observed outcomes are different prediction targets.

03 / Evidence, with context

Look past the output.

Concrete comparisons reveal what a report’s format alone cannot tell us.

The metric can reverse the verdict.

On held-out values in a number look-up task, xVal achieves lower numerical error (MSE 0.0035 vs. 0.09), while FP15 more often reproduces the value to three significant figures (34.5% vs. 1.5%). The same evaluated predictions support different rankings.

A compact report can hide extensive computation.

A fitted probe moves teacher sampling offline. RELISH reuses one backbone encoding within a request. CAMEL reflects selectively. Other scalar interfaces aggregate repeated online generations.
